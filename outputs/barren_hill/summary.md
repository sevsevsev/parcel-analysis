# Barren Hill listing: summary

Seven parcels at 323 to 333 Barren Hill Road and 2121 to 2137 Harts Lane, Whitemarsh Township,
Montgomery County. The county parcel lines dissolve into one contiguous piece of 19.0 acres.
The deeds add up to 20.3 acres, so the mapped lines run about 6% short of the paperwork, most
of it on 2129 Harts Lane (1.18 acres mapped against 1.65 recorded). A survey would settle that.

## The shape of the land

This is a north-facing hillside. Sixty-one percent of the ground faces north and another 28%
faces northwest. Two thirds of it is steeper than 15%, and a third is steeper than 25%. The
four Barren Hill Road parcels (323, 325, 331, 333) carry nearly all of the steep ground; the
three Harts Lane parcels at the top of the slope are gentle, mostly 3 to 8%.

Using a 15% slope limit and a 50 ft setback from the outer boundary, 4.0 acres are buildable,
or 21% of the site. Almost all of it is one pad of 3.3 acres spanning the Harts Lane parcels,
roughly 670 by 290 ft with a mean slope of 7%. The Barren Hill Road parcels contribute 0.7 acres
between them, in scattered patches too small to build on. If the township allows building on
slopes up to 25%, the buildable total rises to 9.0 acres, though that ground would need cut
and fill. Whitemarsh has a steep slope ordinance, and the slope limit and setback in this
analysis are placeholders until someone reads it.

## Sun

The buildable pad gets about 8.1 of a possible 8.8 hours of direct sun on December 21, because
it sits at the top of the hill with nothing to the south to shade it. A winter solar index that
accounts for the angle of the sun on the slope puts the pad at 0.82 relative to flat open
ground. The hillside parcels score around 0.5. Only 4% of the whole property faces south or
is flat. The model uses a bare-earth elevation surface; the site is wooded, so real winter sun
on the ground will be less than this until trees come down.

## Water

About 5.8 acres of neighbouring land uphill drains onto the property, and roughly 1,500 ft of
concentrated flow paths run down the hillside parcels. Runoff leaves at five points along the
Barren Hill Road frontage, the largest carrying 2.9 acres of drainage at 325 Barren Hill Road.
Nothing ponds: the only depression found is 0.015 acres. The site is not in a FEMA flood zone
and has no mapped wetlands or streams on it.

The soils favour infiltration. The hillside is Parker gravelly loam, hydrologic group A and
somewhat excessively drained; the Harts Lane parcels are Gladstone gravelly loam, group B,
well drained. There are no hydric soils and the shallowest restrictive layer is 168 cm down.
Stormwater management for a house on the pad would be straightforward on this ground. The
exposure to manage is the water arriving from uphill of the Harts Lane parcels and the erosion
risk of disturbing the steep face below them.

## Growing

Judged for cultivation instead of construction, with no setback, the picture improves. There are
3.3 acres of garden-grade ground: slope under 8%, eight or more hours of June sun, dry soil.
Most of it is on the Harts Lane parcels, where the soil is Gladstone gravelly loam, which the
USDA rates as prime farmland. Another 5.5 acres suit an orchard or terraced beds, on slopes up
to 20%, and 3.6 acres would do as pasture or meadow. The hillside turns out to have benches of
gentler ground running along the contours between the steep bands, so the orchard and pasture
ground is spread across all four Barren Hill Road parcels rather than confined to the top.

Of the garden and orchard ground, 4.8 acres lie outside the buildable area. Counting building, growing and grazing together, 12.4 acres,
or 65% of the property, can be put to some use. The remaining 6.5 acres are the steep wooded
bands, which could still carry a woodlot or foraged crops. The Parker soils on the hill are
somewhat excessively drained and gravelly, so vegetables there would need water and organic
matter; the USDA rates the 8 to 15% band as farmland of statewide importance and the steeper
bands as not prime.

Sun figures assume the trees are gone. The site is wooded now, and the hillside benches would
need clearing before any of this applies. Soil fertility, stones and deer are not in any public
layer and need a visit and a soil test.

## Zoning, utilities, access

The whole site is zoned AAAA Residential in Whitemarsh, a 90,000 sq ft minimum lot district,
which limits a 19 acre site to about nine lots before any slope or stormwater rules apply.
The ordinance is linked from the zoning table in the full report.

Aqua PA's public water service area covers all of it. Public sewer service areas cover 60%:
the Whitemarsh sewer authority area takes in most of the Barren Hill Road parcels and the
Philadelphia Water Department area touches the Harts Lane end. The Harts Lane parcels where
the building pad sits are the part least covered, so sewer availability there needs a direct
answer from the township.

Road frontage is 801 ft on Barren Hill Road, 421 ft on Harts Lane and 68 ft on Kinder Road.
Barren Hill Road is a PennDOT road, the other two are municipal.

Existing structures: a 1966 colonial of 3,504 sq ft with a 2020 detached garage at 2129 Harts
Lane, and an 1820 colonial of 2,632 sq ft with a 2017 bank barn at 331 Barren Hill Road. A
1965 ranch house on the neighbouring lot crosses the line of 2137 Harts Lane by a sliver, which
is either a mapping error or an encroachment and is worth asking about.

## Reading it as a buyer

The place to build is the three Harts Lane parcels, about 5.2 acres of gentle, sunny,
well-drained prime farmland soil at the top of the hill with a house already on it, and room
for a serious garden beside it. The four Barren Hill Road parcels, 13.8 acres, are a steep
wooded north slope with an old house and barn at the bottom. They hold almost no buildable
land, but about half of them is orchard or pasture grade once cleared, in benches along the
hillside, and the rest is woodlot. Whether the price makes sense depends on how much of that
hillside you would actually clear and work.
