# Port Royal listing: summary

One parcel at 225 Port Royal Avenue, Roxborough, Philadelphia. The city parcel line encloses
3.995 acres with 362 ft of frontage on Port Royal Avenue. There is a two-storey stone house on
it near the road, with a footprint of about 1,700 sq ft, and a 719 sq ft outbuilding. The water
department records 5,115 sq ft of impervious surface on the lot.

## The shape of the land

The lot is a long rectangle running back from the road, and it drops about 90 ft from the
house end to the far corner. Nearly three quarters of it is steeper than 15% and the lower
half is steeper than 25%, where the soil survey maps extremely stony Manor and Chester loams
on 25 to 50% slopes. The slope faces west to south-west, with some south: 86% of the ground
faces between south and west.

At a 15% slope limit and a 25 ft setback, 0.8 acres pass as buildable, and none of it forms a
single patch of a quarter acre. The usable flat ground is where the house already stands, in
the corner by the road. Accepting slopes to 25% raises the figure to 2.8 acres, but that is
hillside that would need cut, fill and retaining walls.

Zoning is RSD-1. The city's Steep Slope Protection Area covers 95% of the lot; under section
14-704(2) of the zoning code it regulates earth moving on steep slopes, so any work beyond the
existing flat area goes through that review. Two other overlays apply, the Fourth District
Overlay and the /NIS overlay, whose provisions should be read from the linked code sections.

## Sun

The west and south-west aspect is good for light. The property averages 7.9 of a possible 8.5
hours of direct sun on December 21, and the winter solar index is 1.17, meaning the slope
catches more low winter sun than flat ground would. The buildable patches score 1.06.

That is the bare-earth figure. The city's canopy map shows trees over 56% of the lot, and most
of the growing ground described below is under them. Clearing would be needed before the sun
numbers apply, and clearing is exactly what the steep slope rules constrain.

## Water

This lot is on the receiving end of its neighbours. About 13.6 acres of land uphill drains onto
it, and the flow map shows a ravine entering from the north-east side and leaving at the
south-west boundary carrying 14.8 acres of drainage. The hydrography dataset maps no stream
here, so this is an unnamed intermittent channel, but it is the dominant feature of the lower
lot and anything built or planted in its path will see concentrated runoff in storms. Nothing
ponds, the soils are group B and well drained, and there is no mapped floodplain or wetland.
Philadelphia Water Department serves the whole city with water and sewer, so no service-area
check was needed; whether the lateral and main are in good order is a question for the seller.

## Growing

Judged for cultivation, the slope is less of a problem than it is for building. Only 0.19 acres
are garden-grade (gentle, sunny, dry), which is the flat corner by the house and the only prime
farmland soil on the lot. But 2.17 acres suit an orchard or terraced beds on slopes to 20%,
and 0.73 acres would do as meadow. Counting building, growing and grazing together, 3.1 acres,
or 78%, are usable in some way, against the 0.8 acres that are buildable. The soils are Manor
loam, well drained and stony, rated farmland of statewide importance on the 8 to 15% band.
About 1.4 of the 2.4 acres of garden and orchard ground are under tree canopy today.

## Reading it as a buyer

This is a house lot with a large, steep, wooded back slope, not a building site. The house
occupies the only flat ground, and the steep slope overlay covers almost everything else, so
the realistic uses of the back three acres are a terraced orchard or garden on the upper slope,
with the ravine left alone, and woodland below. The west-facing aspect gives it good light once
any clearing is approved. The two things to check before going further are the steep slope
review process for any clearing or terracing, and the condition of the ravine after a hard rain.
